SECTION 1.0

Introduction

You are a cadet pilot of the GW-1 Kestrel, a light survey craft working the asteroid fields of Belt Alpha. The Kestrel has no autopilot. Before each flight you work out the forces acting on the ship, then enter the results into the flight computer. The ship flies exactly what you enter.

Every mission is a physics problem with a visible consequence. If your numbers are right, the ship slips through the corridor and crosses the gate. If they are wrong, you see exactly how wrong. Gravity drags you into the rocks, you hit the dock too fast, or you reach a timed gate after it has closed.

1.1What you need

  • A web browser on a computer, tablet or phone. Nothing to install.
  • High-school physics: Newton's laws, kinematics, vectors, momentum, gravity. Each mission introduces one new idea, and the formulas you need are shown in the cockpit (§4.3).
  • Optional: paper and a pencil. Drawing a force diagram before you calculate is the habit of every good pilot.

1.2How to use this handbook

Sections 2–9 explain the cockpit and the rules of flight. Section 10 has a briefing note for every mission. Section 11 collects the physics. Nothing in this handbook gives away a mission's answer; the in-game worked solution does that, at a cost (§9).

NOTE

Open this handbook from the Manual link in the game's top bar. It opens in its own tab, so your mission stays exactly where you left it.

1.3Quick start

  1. On the Sector map, press Start mission on the highlighted level.
  2. Read the Briefing: the situation, the known values, and which values you must enter.
  3. Press Open flight computer. Work out each value, using the calculator if you like, and type it in.
  4. Press LAUNCH and watch the flight.
  5. Clear the gate to earn stars and unlock the next mission. If you crash, read the debrief, fix your numbers and retry.

Every value you enter follows the conventions below. Most first-flight crashes come from a heading pointed the wrong way, so learn Fig 3-1.

FIG 3-1 Heading compass

Heading θDegrees from the +x axis (screen right). Counter-clockwise is positive. 90° is up the screen, 270° is down.
ComponentsFx = F·cos θ (right is positive), Fy = F·sin θ (up is positive).

3.1Corridors and gates

FIG 3-2 Corridor geometry

A corridor is a straight lane through the field with a stated length and width. The ship must stay within half the width of the centreline the whole way. A gate marks the end of the corridor. Some gates are timed (they only open between two times) or speed-limited. A dock is a gate you must arrive at almost stopped. On the orbit mission, the corridor is replaced by a ring-shaped safe band.

4.1Check fields and drive fields

The fields come in two kinds:

Check fields

Intermediate results, such as the size of a gravitational pull or a required acceleration. The ship does not use them, but the debrief grades them so it can find where your working went wrong. Fill them in honestly; they are how the hints work.

Drive fields DRIVES THE SHIP

Marked “drives the ship”. These become the actual burn: thrust, heading, duration, timing. The simulator flies these numbers and nothing else.

WARNING

There is no autopilot and no automatic correction. A right answer in a check field does not rescue a wrong drive field. If a drive field asks for thrust in newtons and you type an acceleration, the engine fires at that tiny value.

4.2Engine limits

Each mission states the engine's maximum thrust. A larger request is clamped to the maximum, so the ship fires at full power instead of the value you typed. If you find yourself entering more than the maximum, re-check your arithmetic; no mission needs it.

4.3Pre-launch checklist

  • Force diagram sketched (every pull, every push)CHECK
  • Units match the field (N, not m/s²; s, not min)CHECK
  • Powers of ten typed with e-notationCHECK
  • Thrust points AWAY from the pull it cancelsCHECK
  • Burn lasts as long as the force it cancelsCHECK
  • Thrust within the engine maximumCHECK
  • Mission assumptions re-readCHECK

SECTION 6.0

Flight & telemetry

After launch the simulator flies your numbers in small steps of a hundredth of a second, applying every force exactly. What you watch is the honest result of your calculation.

6.1Playback controls

ControlFunction
❚❚ / ▶ or SpacePause or resume playback.
1× 4× 16× 64×Playback speed. Long missions are easier to watch at 16× or 64×. Speed does not change the result.
Skip to resultFinishes the flight instantly (or jumps to the next checkpoint).
AbortEnds the flight and returns to the sector map.
+ / −Zoom the viewport.

6.2Live telemetry panel

  • Downrange: distance travelled along the current corridor (on the orbit mission, Arc flown).
  • Drift from centreline: how far off the needed path you are. It turns amber as you near a wall. (Orbit: Radius error.)
  • Speed and Heading: the ship's current velocity.
  • Net force: the sum of every force on the ship right now.
  • Mass: changes if you drop cargo.
  • Forces on ship: each pull (amber) and the thrust (violet), with size and direction.
  • Distance to corridor edge: the margin left before the hull breaches.
  • Fuel: impulse remaining (§7.2).
  • Status line: the physics in plain words, such as “Forces balanced: the ship keeps its velocity”.
NOTE

Watch Net force. If it reads near zero, the ship coasts in a straight line at constant speed (Newton's first law). If it does not, the ship is accelerating, and the status line tells you how hard.

SECTION 7.0

Checkpoints & fuel

7.1Multi-leg missions and checkpoints

Later missions have several legs, each ending in a gate. When the ship crosses a gate that has a checkpoint, the flight pauses and the flight computer reopens with a CHECKPOINT REACHED banner.

  • The Known values now include live telemetry: the ship's actual speed, heading, mass and mission clock at that moment.
  • Plan the next leg from those live values, not from the briefing. The ship rarely arrives at exactly the speed you planned.
  • Press RESUME FLIGHT to continue.
CAUTION

Errors carry forward. A small mistake on leg 1 leaves you a little off speed at the checkpoint. Using the live numbers is how you make up for it.

7.2Fuel

Fuel is measured as impulse: thrust multiplied by burn time, in newton-seconds (N·s). A 200 N burn for 30 s uses 6,000 N·s. Each mission states a fuel budget and a par.

TermMeaning
BudgetThe tank. Run it dry and the thrusters cut out mid-flight; the ship then coasts at the mercy of every pull.
ParThe fuel a well-planned flight uses. Going over par costs a star (§9).
Shared fuelOn the final mission one tank covers all three legs. Wasting fuel early leaves you short later.
NOTE

Some missions use no fuel at all (coasting, a timed clamp, a cargo release). Their fuel gauge is hidden.

SECTION 8.0

Debrief & troubleshooting

When a flight fails, the Crash debrief opens. It shows:

  • The failure, when and where it happened.
  • A replay of your path against the needed path.
  • Your entries: every field marked Correct or with how far off it was (too high, too low, or by how many times).
  • A Hint aimed at your specific mistake, for example spotting that you entered an acceleration where a force was asked for.
  • The Worked solution, hidden until you ask for it. Viewing it caps the mission at 2 stars.

Retry (keep my values) reopens the flight computer at the stage that failed with your numbers still in place, so you only fix what was wrong. Retry from scratch clears everything and starts at stage 1.

8.2When every entry says “Correct” but you still crashed

Answers within a few percent are marked correct, but a tight timed gate can still be missed by a rounding error that builds up over a long leg. Keep at least one more decimal place and fly again. Also re-read the mission assumptions: the burn may need to start, stop or point differently from what you pictured.

SECTION 10

Belt Alpha mission notes

Briefing notes for each mission: the idea it teaches, how it is laid out, and a pilot's tip. No answers.

01 · Launch Window

FORCE & ACCELERATION · F = m·a
LayoutOne corridor, timed gate. One 16-second burn from rest, then coast.
You enterThe acceleration needed, then the engine thrust.
Pilot's tipAcceleration is the change in speed per second. Thrust is the force that produces it on this ship's mass.

02 · Docking Burn

STOPPING DISTANCE · v² = v₀² + 2ad
LayoutFly in toward a dock and brake to 3 m/s or less within 40 m of it.
You enterDeceleration, braking force (fired backward), burn time.
Pilot's tipYou are slowing down the whole way, so d / v gives the wrong time. Brake until the speed reaches zero.

03 · Short Burst

IMPULSE · J = F·Δt = m·Δv
LayoutOne fixed 4-second burst, then coast to a timed gate.
You enterSpeed change, impulse, burst thrust.
Pilot's tipYou are already moving. Impulse buys you the change in momentum, not all of it.

04 · Crosscurrent

VECTORS · Fx = F·cos θ
LayoutA diagonal corridor. Thrust is entered as x and y parts.
You enterHeading, then the x and y components of thrust.
Pilot's tipSketch the triangle formed by the gate offsets. Cosine goes with x, sine with y.

05 · Closing Gap

RELATIVE MOTION · v_rel = v₁ − v₂
LayoutNo burns. Fire a docking clamp as you draw level with a drifting drone.
You enterClosing speed, then the clamp time.
Pilot's tipThe drone keeps moving while you chase it. Think from the drone's point of view.

06 · Pull of KR-6

GRAVITATION · F = G·M·m / r²
LayoutA heavy asteroid below the corridor drags you sideways.
You enterThe pull on the ship, then thrust size, heading and duration to cancel it.
Pilot's tipG·M/r² on its own is an acceleration. A force in newtons needs the ship's mass too. Watch the powers of ten.

07 · Crossfire

VECTOR SUM · ΣF = F₁ + F₂
LayoutTwo asteroids pulling in different directions; one thrust cancels both.
You enterEach pull, the net x and y parts, then thrust size and heading.
Pilot's tipNever add force sizes pointing different ways. Split each pull into x and y, add those, then rebuild one vector with hypot and atan2.

08 · Cargo Drop

MOMENTUM · M·v = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂2 LEGS · CHECKPOINT
LayoutCoast to a drop zone, then eject a cargo pod backward. The recoil speeds you up for a timed gate.
You enterAt the checkpoint: the speed you need, then the pod's ejection speed.
Pilot's tipTotal momentum before the release equals total after. The pod is much lighter than the ship, and the ship's mass is smaller afterward.

09 · Holding Pattern

CIRCULAR ORBIT · v = √(G·M / r)
LayoutReal inverse-square gravity. Speed up into a circular orbit and coast half way round to a pickup gate without leaving the safe band.
You enterOrbital speed, the speed change, and how long to burn at a fixed thrust.
Pilot's tipToo slow and you fall inward; too fast and you climb out. You already have some speed, so only burn for the difference.

10 · The Gauntlet

EVERYTHING3 LEGS · 2 CHECKPOINTS · SHARED FUEL
LayoutLeg 1: cancel one asteroid's pull. Leg 2: a timed gate reached with a short burst. Leg 3: cancel the crossfire of two more asteroids.
You enterA fresh set of values at each checkpoint.
Pilot's tipUse the live speed at each checkpoint. Aim for the middle of a timed window, not its edge. Watch the shared fuel.

11.1Cancelling a force

FIG 11-1 Balanced forces

To fly straight past something that pulls on you, the thrust must be the same size as the pull and point the opposite way (add or subtract 180° from the pull's heading). The net force is then zero and the ship keeps its velocity.

When several bodies pull at once, first add their pulls as vectors (Mission 07), then cancel the total.

A pull keeps acting as long as you are near the body, so the burn must last as long as the pass: t = d / v.

11.2Simulator approximations

To keep each mission about one idea, some missions treat a pull as uniform: constant in size and direction for the whole pass, using the listed distance and bearing. The orbit mission uses true inverse-square gravity. Each briefing states which applies.

Glossary

BurnA period of engine thrust: a force, a heading and a duration.
Check fieldAn input the debrief grades but the ship does not fly (§4.1).
CheckpointA gate where the flight pauses so you can plan the next leg from live telemetry (§7.1).
CorridorThe safe lane through the asteroid field (§3.1).
Drive fieldAn input that becomes the actual burn (§4.1).
DriftSideways distance from the corridor's centreline.
GateThe end of a leg. May be timed, speed-limited, or a dock.
HeadingA direction in degrees from screen-right, counter-clockwise (§3).
ImpulseForce × time, in N·s. Changes momentum; also how fuel is measured.
LegOne corridor of a multi-part mission.
Net forceThe vector sum of every force on the ship.
ParThe fuel a well-planned flight uses (§7.2).
ProgradeIn the direction of travel. Burning prograde speeds you up.
Retro-burnThrust against the direction of travel, to slow down.
Safe bandOn the orbit mission, the ring of radii the ship must stay within.
T+Mission clock: seconds since launch (or since the last checkpoint).
